### Chapter: 1. Defining an Agile Mindset
***********************************************

Not limited to software projects

### Video: Four Agile Values
4 principles of Agile Manifesto (2001) proposed to tackle challenges of a fixed mindset, long project durations, and poor stakeholder satisfaction:
- Individuals and Interactions > Processes and Tools (strictly following pre-defined processes hinders your ability to change and adapt when needed) 
- Working Software > Comprehensive Documentation (if something that does not make your final product better, don't do it)
- Customer Collaboration > Contract Negotiation
- Respond to Change > Follow a Plan (view change as a necessity rather than considering it a waste of time, effort, and resources)

"What students believe about their brains, whether they see their intelligence as something that's fixed or something that can grow and change, has profound effects on their motivation, learning, and school achievement." (Carol Dweck, Stanford psychologist)

### Video: Growth vs. Fixed Mindset
Keys to Growth Mindset:
- Accept mistakes (mistakes provide an opportunity to improve)
- Fail fast to succeed faster (if the product is imperfect- provide feedback and move forward, fail early and spend time improving rather than spending too much time developing, then fail)
- Trust your team (recognize that everyone is learning, keep the communication channel open, and be aware of any life-changing events for your team that may slow down progress)

### Video: The Power of Collaboration
Example of US + France vs. Great Britain in the US's Independence\
The 4 values of the Agile Manifesto are worthless without clear and effective collaboration.

Tips for Team Collaboration:
- Have quick, consistent meetings (expectations are conveyed effectively and ideas are shared, what tasks are being completed, and any roadblocks)
- Make your team's work visible (sticky notes, work visualization software, whiteboard - it does not matter where, but such that everyone sees it so that feedback can be obtained, and ideas can be shared)
- Hear everyone's opinions regularly (employees trust that their ideas will be treated with respect - it takes a long time, agreement, and consistency from everyone)

### Chapter: 2. Agile Mindset Benefits
***********************************************

Knowing your "why" while adopting an agile mindset can make you a true believer.

### Video: Increasing Autonomy
Benefits of Autonomy:
- Trust boosts productivity 
- Encourages learning new skills and connections
- Increases employee retention

Agile makes your work better by increasing autonomy. 

### Video: Changing Organizational Culture
What makes companies much desirable to work for? - their Organizational Culture, which is created by people inside the company.

An agile mindset fosters a positive culture as
- Micromanaging does not co-exist; the manager mentions "what" needs to be done, and the team decides "how"
- With regular meetings within the team and with stakeholders, the scope of miscommunication is less
- Teammates have control over prioritization between their personal and professional life, and overworking 

### Video: Improving Your Delivery
Agile mindset tips to improve workflow:
- No wasting time on presentations, deliver a prototype quickly
- No wasting time on contracts, develop a brief working draft, and gather feedback from stakeholders
- Make a product that your stakeholders need

### Video: Satisfying Your Customers
Two key aspects of customer satisfaction are customer service and price satisfaction.

An agile mindset focuses on: 
- Working efficiently- saving time and money for your organization as well as customers
- Simplifying the delivery process- making work enjoyable and more relatable for stakeholders

### Chapter: 3. Facilitating an Agile Mindset
***********************************************

### Video: Common Pushbacks
People are comfortable doing what they are and struggle to envision what an agile mindset can accomplish.

Personas of people who create pushback in adopting an agile mindset: 
- The Complainer: unwilling to understand agile or leadership has not conveyed the transformation effectively, leading them to voice unhappiness and distress others' adoption of agile consciously or unconsciously.
- The Know-it-All: thinks they have nothing to learn and resists transformation because they do not know the benefits of agile
- The Lost Leader: uses agile as a buzzword but does not know what the agile mindset is (misinformed by assumptions like agile will fix all of our problems quickly, or it can do more work with fewer people)

Agile is a tool that will change the way you work and deliver.  

### Video: Leveraging Change Champions
Example of Lafayette- a French aristocrat who volunteered his services for the US to win a war against the British, who can be considered a 'change champion'.

How to identify and utilize change champions?
- Seek colleagues who are excited to change (e.g., taking an interest, asking questions)
- Put them in contact with others who may have neutral or negative feelings (not necessarily one-to-one), which can help them realize how the transformation might benefit them and the team.
- Ask them to lead meetings and discussions, as they will likely have the most information about the change due to their curious and open nature   

While adopting agile, you need 'change champions' who can be your ally in implementing the transformation. They typically have vision, they fiercely advocate change, they go out of their way to provide support to make change, and willingly ask others to get a little uncomfortable to make it happen. 

### Video: Loving Easy Wins
How to find and use easy wins:
- Divide work into small chunks (easy wins rather than lengthy losses)
- Create a space where everyone can see your work - understand the bigger picture of how you are succeeding and moving forward together
- Celebrate simple achievements - give shoutouts and congratulations to employees to tell them that what they contributed made a difference

### Chapter: 4. Focus on Results
***********************************************

### Video: Tracking Your Results
At its core, the agile mindset is meant to make a measurable impact on your organization; therefore, tracking results is crucial. 

To measure past impact:
- Determine how much time you saved for your team/others 
- Determine how much money you saved for your organization
- Compare your project to other similar projects and have a relative comparison of time/money saved

To measure future impact:
- Measure baseline performance (time/output/money) and keep track of improvements over time
- Performance improvement charts
- Determine metrics to track with your team 
 
### Video: Frequent and Open Communication
Creating an environment with frequent and open communication is essential to deliver positive results.

Communicate with an agile mindset:
- Establish a frequent, open routine to share all appropriate information
- Share positive and negative examples with leadership
- Communicate stories of successful agile transformation to your organization

### Video: Preparing for Setbacks
We often know what the desired transformation looks like, but implementation may not be straightforward (it may be because of employees or management). 

To overcome obstacles in the agile mindset:
- Know others who share your challenges
- Be patient and persistent
- Anticipate and prepare now, be flexible to adapt
